Undergraduate SRTP · Team research project

How do environmental differences shape population dynamics?

Stability and numerical simulation of a predator–prey model with habitat complexity and diffusion

University of Science and Technology Beijing · Undergraduate research training

In this team research project, I contributed to modeling and numerical analysis of predator–prey systems. The study incorporates habitat complexity and diffusion, combining equilibrium-stability analysis with MATLAB simulations of temporal dynamics and spatial patterns.

Question

How do changes in habitat complexity and diffusion affect coexistence, oscillation and spatial distribution?

Method

Formulate ordinary differential and reaction–diffusion models; examine equilibria and eigenvalues; compare parameter scenarios in MATLAB.

Results in context

Compare model behavior using phase portraits, time series and spatial-density plots, illustrating oscillation, convergence and population decline under different settings.

Master's dissertation · Individual research

Do viewing and posting have different later associations?

Longitudinal analysis of social media use and psychological distress among UK adults

The University of Manchester · Social Research Methods and Statistics

Using UKHLS waves 11–15, I paired adjacent waves to construct an analytical sample of 33,027 adults and 91,283 person-period observations. The study examines frequent viewing and posting separately in relation to subsequent psychological distress, alongside socioeconomic differences and alternative specifications.

33,027Adults
91,283Person-period observations
11–15UKHLS waves

Question

Do associations with subsequent distress differ between viewing and posting, and across socioeconomic circumstances?

Method

Use Stata for cross-wave merging, coding and sample construction; account for baseline distress and sociodemographic factors in lagged regressions, interactions and sensitivity analyses.

Results in context

Use sequential models, subgroup comparisons and robustness displays to examine behavior-specific longitudinal associations and their sensitivity to controls, missingness and model choice.
